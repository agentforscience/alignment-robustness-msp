\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proposed \mspfull, the minimum number of training or evaluation decisions that must be flipped simultaneously away from a null-producing baseline before misalignment exceeds a pre-registered threshold, and we decomposed it into an elicitation component and an induction component that separate ``the pathway is unelicited'' from ``the pathway is absent.''

\para{Key findings.} First, nulls are diagnostic, but of the evaluation at least as much as of the model. The 5\%-dilution null is a clean $0/23$ at the baseline evaluation specification and goes to $100\%$ ($8/8$) under a single persona flip --- a textbook masked null, until the base model does the same thing ($0/22 \to 15/16$). Second, and most transferably, the naive elicitation reading is confounded: a single eval-side flip produces a large misalignment rate in the \emph{unmodified} base model, so any procedure that concludes ``the null was masked because a prompt flip recovered misalignment'' is measuring jailbreakability unless it contrasts against the base model at the identical specification. Third, on mechanism the evidence points to a bottleneck rather than redundancy --- effective ranks of $5.83$--$7.59$ against a random-subspace null of $14.9$ --- though provisionally, since the mediation model could not be estimated and the benign-finetune control was never trained. Fourth, equivalence-testing misalignment nulls is practical: two induction cells here are licensed at $\tau$ with one-sided upper bounds of $10.5\%$ and $8.3\%$.

\para{What we did not establish.} \mspi, the interaction decomposition, and the mediation model are not identified. The gap between the design and the delivered result is almost entirely one resource --- judge throughput, which ran ${\approx}2.3\times$ below benchmark and left $43$ of $64$ cells scored. The design is sound and the code runs end to end; it needs roughly $5$--$10\times$ the scoring budget, or a faster judge, to produce the numbers it was built to produce.

\para{Future work.} The highest-value next steps, in order: re-score the identical generations with \gptfouro and with a second open judge, since $\tau$ and therefore \msp are judge-dependent and this is the largest untested threat; add aligned-model controls across the whole evaluation lattice and report \mspa as the primary quantity; replace the binary \mix factor with a $0/5/25/50/100\%$ dose to locate the threshold rather than bracket it; bring every lattice cell to at least three seeds; test whether a specification's \msp transfers across model families or is a property of the base model's alignment training; and measure redundancy directly by ablating the misalignment axis and counting how many orthogonal directions must be removed to drive misalignment below $\tau$, rather than approximating it with effective rank.

\para{Takeaway.} A null result in misalignment training cannot be diagnosed by evaluation-side elicitation alone. Without a base-model control at the identical specification, ``the misalignment was there all along'' and ``we jailbroke an ordinary model'' are the same measurement.
